# Capstack tone of voice

**In one line:** we sound like the sharp, kind older cousin who works in finance. They sit with you at the shop counter, ask
easy questions, never make you feel small, and tell you straight what your business is worth.

**The feeling we leave:** *I understand this. I can do this. These people are on my side.*

---

## Five rules

### 1. Say what happens, then why it's easy
Every title tells the owner what they'll actually do or get. The cleverness, if any, goes in the second line.
- ✅ "Tell us what you do" · "Three minutes, one easy question at a time."
- ❌ "Tell us your story" (story of what? how long? what for?)

### 2. Take the worry out before it starts
Owners worry about time, being judged, finance words, and their data. Answer those quietly, with specifics.
- Say how long: "about three minutes", "two minutes".
- Say what's okay: "Rough guesses are fine." "Skip anything and come back later."
- Say who sees it: "Only you, until you choose to share it."
- ❌ Never "Simply…" or "Just…" before something that might be hard for them.

### 3. Their words, not ours
Talk like a shop owner talks, not like an analyst.

| Say | Not |
|---|---|
| what your business is worth, its worth, value | valuation, number, figure, EV |
| sales | revenue, turnover |
| what's left for you | profit, net margin, EBITDA |
| what you owe | liabilities |
| how sure we are | confidence score, accuracy |
| buyers pay about 2 years of what's left | 2.0× multiple (fine in the breakdown, with the plain version beside it) |
| your file | your account, your profile, your dashboard (dashboard only where it is literally the screen) |

### 4. Warm and a little playful, never jokey
One small smile per section is plenty: a friendly turn of phrase, not a pun. The owner's business is serious to them,
so the joke is never about their money or their effort.
- ✅ "Rent, stock, the generator, everything." · "Got a shoebox of POS slips? Even better."
- ❌ Slang overload, forced pidgin, exclamation marks, emoji in product copy.

### 5. Confident, not salesy
We state things plainly and show the working. No hype, no promises we can't keep.
- ✅ "Every reason is written down." · "Same answers, same result, every time."
- ❌ "Revolutionary", "unlock your potential", "AI-powered insights", "guaranteed".

### 6. Two readers at once
Every line has to work for a broker and for a first-time owner. Be **precise underneath and plain on the surface**:
name the real method, then say it in everyday words.
- ✅ "We use the method brokers use for small businesses: what's left for you each year, times what buyers pay in your trade."
- ✅ "Adjusted for the extra risk of doing business in Nigeria."
- ❌ Vague comfort with no substance ("smart AI works it out"). ❌ Jargon with no plain version ("SDE × sector multiple").
- A broker should finish the page thinking *they know valuation*. An owner should finish it thinking *I understand, and I know what happens next*.

---

## Shape of our copy
- **Titles:** short, plain, verb first where it's a step ("Add your sales and costs"). Sentence case. A full stop on section headlines only.
- **Body:** one or two short sentences. Lead with the benefit or the reassurance, then the detail.
- **Buttons:** what you get, in your voice: "See what mine is worth", "Join the waitlist". Not "Submit", not "Learn more".
- **Numbers on the page:** always in Naira, rounded (₦24M, not ₦24,371,000), always a range for a business's worth.
