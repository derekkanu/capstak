Capstack

What your business is worth, in plain words. A guided valuation for African small businesses that ends in a number you can defend, and later, a marketplace of businesses ready for investment.

A guide you chat with helps an owner value their business on their phone, without a consultant and without knowing finance. The AI gathers the story; fixed, documented rules do the maths.

Why it exists

Owners decide without a number

Cost

A consultant's valuation costs thousands of dollars. Most SME owners will never pay that.

Language

Global tools assume spreadsheets and finance words: EBITDA, net margin, discount rate.

Context

Nothing is built for Naira, informal records, mixed personal and business money, or phone-first owners.

Market figures in the current pitch (SME counts, failure rates, market size, competitor names) still need sources before they go in front of investors.

The business

Three tiers, one engine

Each tier is the same engine with more of it switched on. Free gets people in; owners pay once; brokers pay monthly and make the whole thing more accurate.

One long chat is a long form in disguise. So onboarding only collects the basics and gets the owner to their dashboard fast. The deeper work (how the business makes money, the numbers, documents) lives on the dashboard as tasks they do at their own pace. When enough is in, the dashboard unlocks the next stage: the valuation.

Principle

Every task pays something back

Onboarding → a clean business profile and a first rough range. Business model → a one-page canvas they can show a bank. Numbers and documents → a sharper range and higher confidence. Stopping halfway still leaves the owner with something useful.

The core idea

Questions in everyday language

This is the moat. Owners answer accurately when the question matches how they already think about their shop, and they learn the finance without noticing. They can always ask "what do you mean?" and the guide explains in plain words.

Chapter 2

The business model, in plain questions

The Business Model Canvas has nine boxes. Capstack asks each one as a normal question, fills in what earlier answers already told it, and lets the owner finish the rest later. The important part: most boxes change the value, because they show risk.

Key partners

"If your main supplier stopped selling to you, would you be in trouble?"→ supplier risk · multiple ±

Key activities

"What do you do every day that keeps the business running?"→ owner dependence

Value proposition

"Why do customers buy from you and not the shop next door?"→ report story, edge

Customer relationships

"Do the same customers come back? How often?"→ repeat revenue · multiple ±

Customer segments

"Who buys from you most? Is one customer a big share of sales?"→ concentration risk · multiple ±

Key resources

"What could you not run without? Equipment, a licence, a location, a person?"→ asset value + key-person risk

Channels

"How do people find you and pay you? Walk-in, WhatsApp, Instagram, transfer?"→ often auto-filled

Cost structure

"What are your biggest costs each month? Which ones stay the same even in a slow month?"→ fixed vs variable · income method

Revenue streams

"What are all the ways money comes in? Which is the biggest?"→ diversification · comparison + income methods

The engine

Run every method the data allows, then blend by confidence

No single method is best for every African SME; it depends on the records. So the engine routes. It runs each method only when it has enough to stand on, weighs each by how good its inputs are, and shows one range with a confidence score.

Same answers, same result

The AI never invents the number. It only gathers and explains. The rules are fixed and documented, which is what brokers and banks need to trust it.

Risk moves the number

Business-model answers lower or raise the multiple (comparison) and the discount (income). One big customer or one supplier = riskier = lower value.

Africa-specific inputs

Naira and local benchmarks, inflation in the income method, adjustments for informal records and owners who mix personal and business money.

Confidence

Missing data becomes the next step

Instead of failing when records are thin, the engine says how sure it is and what would make it surer. That is the switching logic made visible, and it is also the natural upgrade path.
